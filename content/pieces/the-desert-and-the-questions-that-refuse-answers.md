At night the flat set its facts out around him. The fan stumbled, as it did. The stain held its shoreline. The drawer held its letters, addressed to nobody, to everybody, to versions of himself he might never meet. He had stopped confusing looking back with looking ahead. What he had learned was simpler: you carry on, and carrying on is a verb in its own right.

When sleep didn't arrive on time, he let his mind try the thoughts that daytime doesn't permit because they take up too much room. He let them move slowly, like animals that startle easily.

He began with an observation he had earned by living. Human beings are partly made by the eyes of other human beings. We learn the outline of ourselves from the look of welcome, from the surface of someone else's hunger, from the small repairs lovers make in us when they say what they want. Nobody had met him that way. He had been assessed, appreciated for being reliable, sympathised with, and refused: efficiently, irritably, pityingly. None of those looks is the look that confirms a body as wanted.

He didn't blame anyone. He could have made a list of failures, but lists are poor instruments for music. He tried to ask a cleaner question instead. If a person is never desired, what changes in him? It was a question about the body and about something beyond it. He could feel the answer, but he couldn't say it.

He tried images. A violin left on a shelf is still a violin. It can be tuned, dusted, insured. It doesn't turn into a chair because nobody plays it. It stays an instrument in a desert. But wood warps, and strings slacken, and the room forgets that sound needs a bow. The instrument becomes honest about its silence. He held on to this for a minute and let it go, because an instrument always has a hand somewhere in the world waiting for it. People don't.

He thought about history. People are remembered for the wars they won, the bridges they built, the loves they made others witness. Great romances produce smaller ones. Letters end up in museums. Photographs in private albums outlast their subjects. He could picture a future in which a landlord went through his papers because he needed the drawer for the next tenant. A stamp with a bird on it would fall to the floor and be swept into a bag. He wasn't afraid of that. He was almost relieved at how accurate it was.

He thought about biology. Desire is a current that asks the body to come forward. He had felt that current, and then felt it slow down until it was a lake with no streams. He didn't moralise about this. He wasn't a monk who wanted sainthood. He was a citizen of a quiet country with a passport that expired on time. He wondered sometimes whether bodies that don't receive certain looks find another circuit for the excess. He noticed he cooked more carefully once the weather cooled. He noticed he cleaned the windows with more attention after festival days. He noticed he took longer to square the corners of the bedsheet. He used care where other people use ardour. He didn't call that a victory. He called it substitution.

He thought about the word *unwanted* and found it too loud. It suggested a decision made by a committee. He didn't believe anyone had met to vote on him. He preferred *unchosen*. It accounted for the absence without inventing malice. It left room for the fact that everyone lives inside a distribution they didn't design. He hadn't been chosen for games, for notes, for names said softly, for the brief madness that couples attempt and sometimes survive. He had been chosen for tasks, for laughter in groups, for competence under irritation. These aren't nothing. He respected them.

He went back to the platform image. Loneliness as a delay. Desolation as the timetable that never had your train in it. He found comfort in the difference because it stopped him from bargaining. Bargaining wears down dignity. He wanted to keep his.

Sometimes his thoughts turned sharp and asked whether he had been a coward. He gave the question its place in the room. He had confessed three times. He had been refused three times, in three different flavours: puzzled, irritated, gentle. After the third, wanting had started to look ridiculous, so he'd stopped. If that was cowardice, he could live with it. Courage sometimes looks like setting a glass down gently on a table instead of smashing it against the wall to prove walls exist.

He wondered whether other people like him were scattered across the city, each performing a life with the right gestures, each doing the small hygienic acts that keep a country from sticking together. He liked to imagine them, because it gave him a sense of brotherhood that didn't require meeting anyone. Once he wrote to them on a sheet. *Dear Others Who Will Not Be Named, we are not a movement. We are a mineral. We exist and add weight to the earth.*

There was one more question he allowed himself, as a treat. A man who is never desired: is he free of some fears, or trapped in others? He had no answer. He lay there listening to the fan find its flaw, lose it, and find it again. The repetition was oddly restful. If the fan could keep going, so could he.

He thought about the baby he'd seen at the tea stall, laughing at a spoon that had caught the light. He thought about the old woman on the ground floor who sat out every afternoon with her feet in a basin of warm water, going cloudy with relief. He thought about the stray dog that knew exactly when a certain man would drop half a biscuit. All of them proved the world wasn't mocking him. All of them also proved it could be sweet without him. He found he could hold both at once, and neither argued with the other.

He thought about the letters. They weren't a cry for help, he told himself. They were a record. Someone might read them one day, after the room had been cleared, and think: this man took himself seriously enough to write it down. He smiled at his own vanity and let it be. It wasn't hurting anyone.

He went back to the word he had said in the power cut. Desolation. He said it now without any moonlight. It hadn't lost its shape. It didn't dramatise him. It named a landscape and let him walk across it without pretending he saw water where there wasn't any.
