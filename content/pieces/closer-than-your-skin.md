I've seen everything,

I've seen everyone.

The ones who wear me,

Rarely seen me in return.

  


I've seen a little boy reading his first words,

Another girl seeing her mother for the first time.

I've seen a teen falling in love,

I've seen heart breaks,

And passion.

  


I've caught shimmers of tear unspoken,

And sparkles of joy mid-laughter.

I've felt the heat of secrets shared,

And of sweats of despair.

  


I've heard secrets that shatter,

I've listened to romances oozing love.

I've listened to silences louder than screams,

Watched cheeks flushing with a single glance.

  


I've sat on the noses of the bold and the brave,

And those with trembling faces afraid to hope.

I've torn down with the weight of grief,

And tilted with joy they couldn't cope.

  


I’ve fogged not from the rain, but heat,

Of stolen kisses in backseat nights.

I’ve tilted as they leaned in close,

Hearts syncing in reckless flights.

  


I've heard whisper, “can they see me?”,

And the answer lived inside their eyes.

I was there when the truth was almost spoken,

Then buried beneath polite goodbyes.

  


And though they clean me, drop me, lose me,

They blame me when the world goes blur.

They never ask me what I remember,

Whether the ghosts of their past still stir.

  


And yet, I hold each fleeting flame,

Each heavy breath, each whispered moan.

For though I never touch or taste—

I’ve never been entirely alone.

  


So clean me, wear me, toss me down,

But know—I see the truths you hide.

I’ve watched your boldest, softest selves,

When no one else was by your side.
